# Batch and Series Generation

Use this reference when creating 3 or more related articles, posts, landing pages, guides, or other pieces in one batch or series.

## Core principle

Plan the batch as a corpus before drafting individual pieces. Do not generate each item independently and accept the aggregate structure afterward.

## Batch planning matrix

Define internally for each piece:

- topic;
- primary reader task;
- editorial thesis;
- content function;
- dominant content shape;
- evidence burden;
- publication boundary;
- modules genuinely needed;
- modules explicitly omitted;
- topics to LINK-OUT;
- likely overlap with sibling pieces.

Content functions may include diagnostic, mechanism explanation, comparison, workflow, opinion/argument, reference-oriented guide, case analysis, measurement methodology, implementation overview, troubleshooting, or strategic decision guide.

Do not assign different structures merely to create variety. Reader task, evidence, subject mechanics, and genre determine structure.

## Drafting order

1. Establish the batch's shared editorial standard and scope.
2. Resolve overlap, ownership, and LINK-OUT boundaries in the matrix.
3. Establish evidence identity for precise claims that may recur across pieces using `evidence-gate.md`.
4. Draft the pieces against their individual reader tasks.
5. Audit the completed corpus, not just each artifact.

## Batch fingerprint hard gate

For batches of 5 or more related pieces, explicitly inspect corpus architecture before completion. Look for a non-essential dominant pattern such as:

- mini-framework → Contents → numbered sections → CTA → Sources → FAQ;
- Executive summary → 3–5 bullets → Contents → numbered sections;
- Key takeaways at the top of nearly every piece;
- N numbered principles, FAQ, checklist, or CTA in nearly every piece;
- Sources + CTA as the universal ending;
- a product section in the same location every time;
- identical heading grammar, section counts, opening cadence, or conclusion shape.

If a non-functional pattern dominates most of the batch, treat it as a **batch planning failure**. Do not repair it by randomly moving modules. Instead:

1. reconsider whether each repeated module is necessary;
2. integrate useful repeated material where it naturally belongs;
3. omit modules without an independent reader job;
4. re-plan pieces whose reader tasks support a different architecture;
5. preserve similarity where genre or function genuinely requires it.

Natural similarity is acceptable. Unexplained systemic sameness is not.

## Batch amplification protection

Repetition is not validation. A threshold, benchmark, timing window, crawler behavior, platform rule, or recommendation must have an established evidence identity before it propagates across the batch.

Do not let one unsupported assertion become an apparent industry standard because several sibling pieces repeat it. Product-defined rules must remain visibly product-owned; illustrative values must remain examples; measurements retain their cohort and conditions.

## Pass condition

The batch has a coherent editorial standard, but each piece's structure follows its reader task. Selective modules remain selective. Overlap is deliberate, evidence identity survives reuse, and no random structural novelty was introduced merely to look different.
