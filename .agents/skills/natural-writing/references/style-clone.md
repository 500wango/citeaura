# Style Clone

## Goal

Match observable writing behavior from user-provided samples without inventing biographical or psychological explanations for that style.

## Minimum evidence

- Prefer 3–5 samples totaling at least several hundred words.
- If only 1–2 short samples exist, imitate cautiously and avoid claiming a stable style profile.
- Prefer samples from the same medium as the target output when available.

## Build the profile

Extract a compact profile across these dimensions:

1. **Sentence rhythm** — typical sentence length, variance, fragments, long cumulative sentences.
2. **Paragraph rhythm** — short versus dense paragraphs, one-line emphasis, spacing.
3. **Register** — conversational, technical, formal, blunt, playful, restrained.
4. **Directness** — how quickly the writer states the point; degree of hedging.
5. **Transitions** — explicit connectors versus implicit jumps.
6. **Evidence style** — examples, numbers, analogies, citations, anecdotes.
7. **Rhetorical habits** — questions, contrast, repetition, parentheticals, lists.
8. **Lexical habits** — recurring non-sensitive vocabulary, preferred verbs, avoided clichés.
9. **Openings and endings** — typical entry and exit patterns.
10. **Formatting** — headings, bullets, punctuation, emphasis.

## Apply the profile

- Match distributions, not catchphrases. Do not over-repeat distinctive words merely to signal imitation.
- Preserve the target text's facts and purpose.
- Keep domain terminology that the target requires even if it is absent from the samples.
- Prefer the author's recurring structural habits over superficial vocabulary mimicry.
- If samples conflict, follow the subset closest to the target medium.

## Do not infer

Do not infer or state the author's age, gender, ethnicity, nationality, health, politics, personality type, intelligence, or other personal attributes from prose style.
