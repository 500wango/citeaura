Understanding AI crawlers and directives

AI crawlers serve different roles. Blocking all bots stops generative search engines from referencing your official documentation and comparison facts.

Search & Retrieval

GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot

Used by Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, and Claude Web Search to fetch fresh content during real-time user inquiries. Blocking these bots removes your site from live citations.

User-Directed Fetching

ChatGPT-User

Triggered when an end-user explicitly pastes a URL or asks ChatGPT to summarize a specific webpage. Requires HTTP 200 access to public routes.

Training Control

Google-Extended, Bytespider

Controls whether public content is ingested into baseline training sets (such as Gemini or Doubao). Many sites allow search crawlers while disallowing training-only bots.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI crawlers does this tool test?

This tool inspects directives for GPTBot (OpenAI retrieval), ChatGPT-User (browsing), ClaudeBot and Anthropic-AI (Anthropic), PerplexityBot (Perplexity AI), Bytespider (Doubao/ByteDance), and Google-Extended (Google Gemini training control).

Does unblocking an AI crawler guarantee AI citations?

No. Crawler access is a necessary prerequisite for retrieval, but citations depend on entity disambiguation, factual density, passage extractability, and model perception.

How do I safely allow AI search engines without exposing private data?

Declare specific Disallow rules for internal application paths (such as /api/, /admin/, /account/) while keeping public product, documentation, and blog routes accessible under Allow: /.
