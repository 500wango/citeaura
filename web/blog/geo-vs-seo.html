GEO vs SEO: What Changes and What Stays

GEO adds the task of measuring how your brand appears in generated answers. SEO still provides much of the foundation: accessible pages, useful information, and a site people can trust. The practical difference is in the evidence you collect and the decisions it supports.

Updated 12 September 2026 · About 7 min read · Strategic analysis

By Elena Torres

GEO vs SEO: the difference is what you measure

Search engine optimization helps people find and use your pages through search. Generative engine optimization focuses on how your brand and content appear in generated answers: whether a system mentions you, describes you accurately, and links to a useful source. These objectives overlap. A page can earn search visits without appearing in an AI answer, and an answer can mention a brand without sending it a click.

For a marketing team, that changes the questions asked of an existing content program. Search position and organic conversions remain useful. Alongside them, you need to inspect the answers produced for relevant buyer questions. Does the answer include your category? Does it associate your product with a feature you actually sell? Is the supporting link yours, a review site’s, or absent?

The term GEO was introduced in the research paper GEO: Generative Engine Optimization. Its benchmark provides evidence about the techniques tested in that research setting. It does not establish a universal ranking formula for today’s ChatGPT, Google, or Perplexity products, and its reported gains are not a forecast for an individual website.

How the reporting questions differ
QuestionSEO evidenceAI visibility evidence
Can people find us?Indexing, impressions, query positions, clicks.Recorded mentions and citations for a defined set of questions.
What do they see?Search results and the landing page.The complete answer, product description, and supporting sources.
Did it help the business?Qualified visits, leads, purchases, and other agreed outcomes.Observed answer changes, plus attributable visits and conversions where available.
What can we change?Website access, information quality, navigation, and relevance.Those same inputs, informed by gaps found in actual answers.

The foundations that stay useful

A readable, accurate, accessible website remains the starting point. GEO does not justify replacing useful SEO work with a separate set of supposed tricks for models.

Google makes this explicit in AI features and your website:

“There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary.”

That statement applies to Google’s AI search features. Google also says a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet to qualify as a supporting link. Eligibility is a prerequisite, not a promise that the page will be selected.

The practical work is familiar: publish substantive information, use descriptive titles, connect related pages with crawlable links, and make important text available on the page. A broken product page is still a broken product page, whether the reader arrived through a search result, an AI citation, or a bookmark.

Content quality also includes maintenance. If pricing, limits, or supported integrations change, update the pages where those facts are stated. Adding more pages while leaving contradictory product claims in place creates more information for readers to reconcile. Fixing the contradiction is often the more useful task.

What AI answers add to the work

Inspect the answer, not just the destination

An AI answer may summarize several sources and attach links to specific claims. Google documents that AI Overviews and AI Mode may issue multiple related searches through a technique it calls query fan-out. That gives a reason to examine the range of questions a page answers. It does not tell us the private scoring rules used to choose every citation.

Suppose your product supports export to CSV, but an answer says it supports direct synchronization with a data warehouse. The brand mention is present; the description is wrong. A mention-rate chart alone would miss the problem. Save the answer, inspect its cited sources, and compare the claim with current documentation before deciding which page to change.

Separate mentions, citations, and visits

A mention is the appearance of a brand name or a recognized alias. A citation is a source link attached to the answer. A visit is a user action recorded by your site analytics. None is a substitute for the others.

A useful source page might be cited without its publisher becoming a recommended product. Conversely, an answer might recommend a product while citing an independent review. Decide whether the project aims to correct product facts, increase inclusion in relevant recommendations, earn source links, or generate qualified visits. The measure should follow that decision.

Record how the answer was obtained

A provider API call with web search enabled and a conversation in its consumer application are different observations. Model choice, retrieval settings, conversation history, and product behavior can differ. Record the collection method rather than labeling every response with the product name alone.

Use the same questions and comparable settings for repeat measurements, and keep the raw answers. Our ChatGPT brand-mention measurement guide explains how to define a question set and keep API measurements distinct from observations in the product interface.

A product page can serve both search and AI readers

Consider a hypothetical analytics product. Its integrations page says, “Connect everything and put your data to work.” A buyer asking whether it can export event data still cannot determine the format, plan requirement, or setup process. The problem is missing information, not an insufficient number of keywords.

A clearer version might read:

Illustrative copy for a fictional product: “Northstar exports event data as CSV files from the Reports page. Scheduled exports are available on the Team plan. Direct warehouse synchronization is not currently supported.”

The revision answers the buyer’s question, names the relevant interface, and states a material limit. It could be followed by a short setup procedure or an example file. Each addition earns its place by helping the reader complete the export, rather than by pushing the page toward a target length.

Use the same verified facts in the product page, help center, and any relevant structured data. Google’s structured-data policies require marked-up information to reflect visible page content. JSON-LD should describe the page you actually publish; it is not a place to add unsupported features or endorsements.

After publishing, check that the public URL contains the updated explanation. Then collect another comparable set of answers. If the description becomes accurate, report that observation with its scope. Do not assume that a citation increase, a ranking change, or a sale must follow from the edit.

Where GEO advice becomes misleading

There is no general requirement to publish an AI-specific file. The llms.txt proposal describes a way to provide information for agents. It can be a useful curated guide to a site, but it is not evidence that a provider has read, indexed, or cited those links. Google explicitly says new AI text files and special schema are not needed for its AI search features.

Search crawling and model training are separate decisions. OpenAI’s crawler documentation distinguishes OAI-SearchBot, used for search, from GPTBot, associated with potential training use. A recommendation to “allow AI bots” is incomplete unless it identifies which activity the site owner intends to permit.

Longer is not automatically more useful. Google’s helpful-content guidance explicitly rejects the idea of a preferred word count. A comparison deserves enough detail to explain tradeoffs. A narrow troubleshooting answer may need only a short procedure. Expand a thin article when important questions are unanswered, not to satisfy a supposed ranking threshold.

How to allocate the next sprint

Start with the strongest observed problem. If essential pages cannot be fetched, investigate access. If answers repeat outdated product details, reconcile the public facts. If the pages are accurate but omit the buyer’s question, add an explanation that addresses it. If the only evidence is one missing mention, collect a better baseline before commissioning a rewrite.
