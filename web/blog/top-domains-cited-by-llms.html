The 20 Domains LLMs Cite Most (and What It Means for Your Brand)

When ChatGPT recommends your competitor instead of you, the cause is rarely product quality. These seven evidence gaps explain the difference — and each one has a concrete fix.

Direct answer: Semrush analyzed 325,000 AI search prompts and found that Reddit, LinkedIn, Wikipedia, YouTube, Medium, and Facebook are the most-cited domains in LLM answers — all user-generated or reference platforms. No brand website appears in the top 20. AI citations happen on third-party platforms, not on company websites.

The top six are all user-generated or reference platforms. Not a single brand website appears in the top 20. The internet's most-cited sources in AI answers are places where other people talk — not places where companies talk about themselves.

Read the fine print first

Before drawing conclusions, three caveats matter.

1. The metric is response share, not citation share. "11.29%" means roughly 11% of AI responses contained at least one Reddit citation — not that 11% of all citations point to Reddit. Different studies slice this differently, and the numbers move with the denominator.

2. These shares are volatile. Semrush's own tracking showed Reddit's citation share on ChatGPT collapsing from nearly 60% in early August 2025 to around 10% by mid-September 2025. A snapshot from January–February 2026 is a data point, not a permanent ranking. Treat Reddit as a leading source, not a guaranteed one.

3. The prompt sample shapes the result. The 325,000 prompts covered 12 industry categories across three AI search tools. A B2B-heavy sample naturally surfaces LinkedIn; a health-heavy sample would surface nih.gov and PubMed. Your category's citation map may look different — which is exactly why brand-level measurement beats industry averages.

What this means for brands

You cannot SEO your way into AI citations from your own domain alone. The classic playbook — publish on your blog, rank it, earn the click — assumes the citation happens on your property. The data says it mostly doesn't. Citations happen on Reddit threads, LinkedIn posts, Wikipedia entries, and YouTube videos.

Presence beats ranking. The practical question is no longer "how do we rank #1 for this query" but "where do the models look when answering this query, and are we there?" For most B2B categories, that means genuine participation on Reddit, a real LinkedIn content presence — individual creator posts earn the citations on ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, not just company pages — and accurate third-party profiles.

LinkedIn is the mover to watch. At #2 overall, LinkedIn sits ahead of Wikipedia and YouTube, and tracking firm Profound calls its rise the largest authority shift it has observed this year, climbing from around #11 to #5 on ChatGPT in just a few months. For professional queries specifically, Profound found LinkedIn to be the #1 most-cited domain across every platform it examined. If you sell to businesses, LinkedIn is no longer optional in a GEO strategy.

Wikipedia is citation-rich and link-poor. It ranks #3 here, yet every Wikipedia outbound link is nofollow — zero PageRank passes. This is the clearest illustration of the GEO/SEO split: Wikipedia does nothing for your Google rankings and a great deal for your AI visibility. Optimize for being cited, not just linked.

What this means for measurement

If citations come from third-party platforms, then rank tracking on your own domain misses most of the picture. The metric that matters is citation share: how often your brand appears in AI answers, on which platforms, and in what context. A traffic chart can't show you that. Repeated sampling across prompts can — which is why any serious GEO program starts with measurement, not tactics.

Frequently asked questions

Why are there no brand websites among the most-cited domains?

Large language models favor third-party, information-dense sources when answering questions: community discussions, reference works, and professional content. A brand's own site is one voice about itself; Reddit threads, LinkedIn posts, and Wikipedia entries aggregate many voices, which models treat as stronger evidence.

Is Reddit's lead in AI citations permanent?

No. Semrush's own tracking showed Reddit's citation share on ChatGPT collapsing from nearly 60% in early August 2025 to around 10% by mid-September 2025. Citation shares are volatile — treat any single snapshot as a data point, not a permanent ranking.

Does getting a Wikipedia page help my Google rankings?

Not directly: every Wikipedia outbound link is nofollow, so zero PageRank passes. But Wikipedia ranks #3 among LLM-cited domains, so a Wikipedia presence does a great deal for AI visibility. It is the clearest illustration of the GEO/SEO split: optimize for being cited, not just linked.
